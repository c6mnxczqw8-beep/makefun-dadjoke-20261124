# Dad joke candidates (draft pass)

Drafted across different pun mechanisms, then scored against the
judge's criteria: clean pun on first read, fresh but still a dad
joke, tight, punchline at the end, natural read-aloud rhythm.

1. Homophone: "The wedding was so emotional, even the cake was in
   tiers." (tiers/tears) -- clean, but a known classic.
2. Double meaning: "I'm terrified of elevators, so I'm taking
   steps to avoid them." -- clean, also well worn.
3. Layered temperature puns: thermostat fight -> heated -> chill
   -> cool. Fresh combination, three payoffs, last word doubles
   as temperature and reconciliation.
4. Literal idiom: "Singing in the shower is fun until you get
   soap in your mouth -- then it's a soap opera." -- good, a bit
   longer to land.
5. Letter-drop: "What do you call a fish with no eyes? A fsh."
   -- pure dad, but told a thousand times.
6. Trick brag: naming the dog "Six Miles" so Dad can say he
   walks Six Miles every day. -- funny, but no real pun turn.
7. Double meaning: "I used to hate facial hair, but then it grew
   on me." -- classic.
8. Workplace pun: scarecrow "outstanding in his field" --
   rejected: the most recycled dad joke there is.
9. Tech personification: smart fridge "feeling a little empty
   inside" -- modern, but the turn is soft.
10. Exercise pun: lunges would be "a big step forward" -- clean,
    single payoff.
11. Book pun: anti-gravity book is "impossible to put down" --
    rejected: stale.
12. Meta: "I'm still working on my construction joke." --
    clever, but more meta-humour than groan.

## Pick

Candidate 3, the thermostat. It is fresh rather than recycled,
the pun chain is understood on the first read, every setup word
pays off, and the punchline word ("cool") arrives last.
